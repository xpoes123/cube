## NISS, slice, and insertion behavior

- **NISS flips per solve: 7.84 mean (elite), 6.83 (long).** The median elite solve has **8 NISS transitions** in the annotated comment — they branch in and out of inverse aggressively to scout EO and DR.
- **50% of elite solves begin EO on inverse** (`(...)` opens the comment). They pre-scan both directions before committing to the EO axis. **0%** of elite solves are fully inverse-only — NISS is a scouting tool, not a one-way flip.
- **Insertions per solve: 0.11 mean in elite vs 0.11 in mid.** Insertions are NOT what separates good from great. They're a recovery tool when the pipeline fails.
- **Skeleton-and-insert flow** ("ab3c", "@1 insert"): elite **0.7%**, long-solve **15.8%**. The modern elite paradigm is **almost no insertions**.
- **Slice moves (M/E/S) in final solution: 0%** at every tier. They are tagged in comments as "// slice" or "// leave slice" but expressed as RL2 / UD2 / FB2 quarter-turn pairs in the final solution. Leave-slice tag appears in **14.2% of elite** comments.
- **Wide moves (`Rw`, `Uw` …) on the HTR→finish line: 191/193 = 99%.** The penultimate phase routinely uses wides for slice/3e2e closure. Uw and Rw dominate.

## Self-reflection corpus (free-text comments)

Keywords frequency across all 899 solves: `found` 152, `missed` 82, `better` 36, `tried` 33, `rewrite` 20. Annotated solves frequently describe rejected branches — "tried 4b2 on UD, saw 4c4e on FB, took the second". The corpus is a sparse record of branch-and-prune search. This is a TEACHABLE behavior our agent does not currently emulate.

## What the failure modes look like (30+ solves)

Five sampled 28-37 move solves all share one of three pathologies:
1. **DR found in 10+ moves, no scouting** — accepted first DR seen; e.g. `DR (15 moves)` followed by a panicked HTR→finish that adds 22 more.
2. **HTR substate is 2e2e + 4c2e** and the finish phase is 8-13 moves (vs 4-6 for sub-22 substates).
3. **Old-style skeleton + 3c insertion** — gets a long skeleton + 2-3 commutator insertions, net 30+ moves.

These are the patterns our agent is currently most similar to.

## Actionable recommendations for the cube agent

1. **The DR-length budget is THE single most-important target.** Sub-22 requires DR ≤ 6 moves with very high probability (85.7%). Sub-25 requires DR ≤ 8.7 on average. If a candidate DR found is ≥10 moves, **reject it and rescan** — that branch will not yield sub-25. Add a hard prompt rule: "DR longer than 9 moves means the EO axis or DR axis choice is wrong; backtrack."

2. **EO is a fixed 4-move budget.** Across every tier — elite, mid, long — EO is ~4 moves. There is no value in spending agent moves on EO > 5; it's free, every scramble has one, and our tool finds them. Drop any EO-recall variant returning 6+ moves.

3. **Always scout EO on both inverse AND normal before committing.** Half of all elite solves start on inverse. Our agent should *always* run the EO finder twice (normal + inverse-scramble) and pick the shorter — this is a single tool call, costs ~zero, and elite humans do it religiously.

4. **NISS is a SCOUTING tool, not a binary flip.** Median elite solve has 8 annotated NISS transitions per solve. Pattern: try DR on inverse → see substate → flip back → finish on normal. Add a tool / prompt that lets the agent **branch-and-discard**: enumerate candidate DRs on both sides and keep the one with the better HTR substate ahead.

5. **Reject `4c4e` triggers when possible.** It's the worst-case DR substate and is over-represented in long solves (26 vs 7 long, vs 33 vs 160 for `4b2` in elite). When the agent sees a `4c4e` DR, prefer scanning the other axis for a `2c3` or `4b2` even if it costs 1-2 EO moves.

6. **Insertions are NOT the path to sub-25.** Elite insertion rate is 0.7%. We should DEPRIORITIZE the 3-cycle insertion tool — it's helping the agent reach 28 but blocking sub-25. Replace its prominence in the prompt with the "rescan DR axis" branch.

7. **Wide moves are the HTR-finish lingua franca.** 99% of elite HTR-finish phases use wide moves (`Uw`, `Rw`). Our finish tool should output wides natively, not just RUF quarter-turns. Specifically: `Uw`, `Rw`, `Fw`, `Dw` are the top four.

8. **Slice moves are written as RL2/UD2/FB2 pairs, never as M/E/S.** Don't emit M/E/S in solutions even when conceptually solving on a slice — the canonical WCA-FMC notation expresses them as quarter-turn pairs (e.g., "L2 R2 U2 D2"). Our tool output is already correct here; document this in the agent prompt so it doesn't get tempted to use M/E/S to "shorten" by 1.

9. **The substate after DR matters more than the DR move count alone.** A 6-move DR landing in `4c4e` is worse than an 8-move DR landing in `2c3`. The agent should rank DR candidates by **(dr_moves + expected_htr_moves_from_substate)**, not by `dr_moves` alone. Use the trigger-distribution numbers above as priors: `2c3 → ~5 HTR moves`, `4b2 → ~6`, `4c4e → ~9`.

10. **Add an explicit "branch journal" to the agent prompt.** Elite annotations literally record rejected branches ("tried 4b2 on FB, found 4a1 on UD in 8, took the second"). Our agent should be told: when you reject a candidate, log it with a one-line reason. This both improves the trace (and the video narrative) and forces the model to *compare* before committing — which is the elite behavior.

## What we tried that didn't pan out / null findings

- **Hoping for axis-selection patterns from annotation.** Explicit axis labels (FB/UD/RL) appear only when the solver is unusual or rotates mid-solve — most don't say. Inferring axis would require running our own DR-axis classifier on the moves. Useful but out of scope for a 30-minute pull.
- **Hoping insertions would teach us something.** Initially expected elite solvers used clever 3-cycle commutators. Reality: insertions are a fallback. The 3.9% of solves with insertion markers are mostly 28+ moves. **Our existing insertion tool is fine; just use it less.**
- **Hoping NISS-flip count would scale with solve quality.** It doesn't really — 7.84 in elite vs 6.83 in long is only a 14% delta. The QUALITY of branching (what substates you scout) matters more than count.
- **Hoping for explicit method labels we could mine for prompt examples.** ~30% of solves don't fit any clean method bucket — solver-specific notation, mid-pipeline switches, hybrid approaches. The labeling is noisier than expected; rely on phase-length statistics, not method labels.
- **Hoping `// EO` distributions would reveal preferred axis.** Of 32 elite solves with axis-tagged EO, distribution is UD:16 / FB:15 / RL:1. RL is avoided but UD vs FB is split 50/50 — so the takeaway is only "avoid RL-EO unless it's clearly shortest," not a strong preference.
